## Political connections and firm value

- **Fisman (2001)**, "Estimating the Value of Political Connections" (`fisman2001estimating`). An event-study design on Indonesian firms, using the stable Suharto-era political structure to build "a credible index of political connectedness". The conclusion is that "a large percentage of a well-connected firm's value may be derived from political connections".
- **Faccio (2006)**, "Politically Connected Firms" (`faccio2006politically`). Cross-country documentation of firms in 47 countries whose controlling shareholders or top officers overlap with legislators or government officials; the prevalence of connections is related to country-level institutional measures, and the announcement of a new political connection is associated with a significant increase in firm value.
- **Faccio, Masulis and McConnell (2006)**, "Political Connections and Corporate Bailouts" (`faccio2006political`). Compares bailout likelihood for 450 politically connected firms from 35 countries over 1997-2002 with matched non-connected peers; connected firms are significantly more likely to be bailed out, especially when the IMF or World Bank assists the home government, and bailed-out connected firms perform worse than bailed-out unconnected ones.
- **Johnson and Mitton (2003)**, *Cronyism and capital controls: evidence from Malaysia* (`johnson2003cronyism`). Compares politically connected and unconnected Malaysian firms through the 1997-98 crisis. The paper attributes roughly 9% of connected firms' estimated $60 billion loss of market value to the fall in the value of connections, and says the September 1998 capital controls mainly benefited firms tied to Prime Minister Mahathir, treating the controls as a screen for supporting those firms. <!-- language-rule-ok: title of a cited work -->
- **Mobarak and Purbasari (2005 version)**, "Political Trade Protection in Developing Countries: Firm Level Evidence from Indonesia" (`mobarak2005political`). An unpublished working paper; firm-level analysis of import licences, described as the major firm-specific trade protection instrument used by the Indonesian government. Politically connected firms are 6 to 22 percentage points more likely to obtain the licences studied.
- **Leuz and Oberholzer-Gee (2006)**, "Political relationships, global financing, and corporate transparency: Evidence from Indonesia" (`leuz2006political`). Using Suharto-era data, firms with strong political connections are less likely to have publicly traded foreign securities, consistent with state-owned domestic banks supplying them cheap capital; the Asian crisis and the change of regime are used to follow connected firms afterwards. Estimates of the performance effect of foreign financing are severely biased if value-creating domestic arrangements such as political ties are ignored.
- **Acemoglu, Johnson, Kermani, Kwak and Mitton (2016)**, "The value of connections in turbulent times: Evidence from the United States" (`acemoglu2016value`). An event study of the November 2008 nomination of Timothy Geithner as Treasury Secretary, comparing financial firms with and without prior connections to him. Financial firms with prior connections to him earned cumulative abnormal returns of about 6% after the first full trading day and about 12% after ten trading days. The paper reads the result as the value of access during a crisis with heightened policy discretion rather than as misconduct, so connections can matter even with strong institutions.

## Ownership in East Asia

- **Claessens, Djankov and Lang (2000)**, "The separation of ownership and control in East Asian Corporations" (`claessens2000separation`). Traces ultimate ownership and control for 2,980 corporations in nine East Asian countries; voting rights often exceed cash-flow rights through pyramids and cross-holdings, most of all in family-controlled and small firms, and more than two-thirds of firms have a single controlling shareholder.
- **Carney and Child (2013)**, "Changes to the ownership and control of East Asian corporations between 1996 and 2008: The primacy of politics" (`carney2013changes`). Compares newly compiled ownership data for 1,386 listed companies at end-2008 with 1,606 at end-1996; where political arrangements persisted, existing ownership arrangements were unchanged or more entrenched, and the state became a more important owner.

## Event-study method

- **MacKinlay (1997)**, "Event Studies in Economics and Finance" (`mackinlay1997event`). A survey of event-study methods, resting on the premise that an event's effect appears immediately in security prices and can be measured over a short window.
- **Brown and Warner (1985)**, "Using daily stock returns: The case of event studies" (`brown1985using`). Simulation evidence on event-study tests with daily data; daily data "generally present few difficulties" for event studies despite non-normality, with attention to non-stationary daily variances.
- **Dimson (1979)**, "Risk measurement when shares are subject to infrequent trading" (`dimson1979risk`). A framework explaining why the classical market model gives biased betas for infrequently traded shares. The method summary is partly from memory (see Not yet verified).
- **Scholes and Williams (1977)**, "Estimating betas from nonsynchronous data" (`scholes1977estimating`). Shows that betas estimated by OLS from nonsynchronously observed daily prices are biased. The method summary is partly from memory.
- **Kolari and Pynnonen (2010)**, "Event Study Testing with Cross-sectional Correlation of Abnormal Returns" (`kolari2010event`). With event-date clustering, even low cross-correlation among abnormal returns causes serious over-rejection; the paper adjusts the Boehmer-Musumeci-Poulsen t-statistic for cross-correlation, and the adjustment extends to multi-day windows.
- **Boehmer, Musumeci and Poulsen (1991)**, "Event-study methodology under conditions of event-induced variance" (`boehmer1991event`). The method is not verified in the notes beyond the title; the Kolari and Pynnönen abstract confirms that their statistic is an adaptation of this one.

## Inference with few clusters or few treated units

- **Cameron, Gelbach and Miller (2008)**, "Bootstrap-Based Improvements for Inference with Clustered Errors" (`cameron2008bootstrap`). Cluster-robust standard errors over-reject with few (roughly 5 to 30) clusters; cluster bootstrap-t procedures bring rejection rates close to the nominal 5% in Monte Carlo experiments, including the Bertrand, Duflo and Mullainathan example.
- **Cameron, Gelbach and Miller (2011)**, "Robust Inference with Multiway Clustering" (`cameron2011robust`). A variance estimator that allows cluster-robust inference under non-nested two-way or multiway clustering, for example by firm and by date.
- **Conley and Taber (2011)**, "Inference with 'Difference in Differences' with a Small Number of Policy Changes" (`conley2011inference`). When only a few groups change policy, the point estimate is not consistent, but its null distribution can be estimated from the many untreated groups; the estimator then serves as a test statistic, and confidence intervals follow by test inversion.
- **MacKinnon and Webb (2017)**, "Wild Bootstrap Inference for Wildly Different Cluster Sizes" (`mackinnon2017wild`). The cluster-robust variance estimator needs a large number of clusters, and the procedure fails when cluster sizes differ greatly or only a few clusters are treated.
- **MacKinnon and Webb (2018)**, "The wild bootstrap for few (treated) clusters" (`mackinnon2018wild`). Proposes the subcluster wild bootstrap, which includes the ordinary wild bootstrap as a limiting case, for inference with few treated clusters.
- **Gelbach, Helland and Klick (2013)**, "Valid Inference in Single-Firm, Single-Event Studies" (`gelbach2013valid`). Normal-theory inference is generally invalid in single-firm event studies; the SQ test uses sample quantiles of pre-event fitted excess returns and has correct asymptotic Type I error (a quantile-of-placebo-days test, not labelled a permutation test in the snippets).

## Common ownership and interlocks

- **Azar, Schmalz and Tecu (2018)**, "Anticompetitive Effects of Common Ownership" (`azar2018anticompetitive`). For US airline routes, taking common ownership into account implies increases in market concentration about 10 times larger than the increase antitrust authorities presume likely to enhance market power.
- **Dennis, Gerardi and Schenone (2022)**, "Common Ownership Does Not Have Anticompetitive Effects in the Airline Industry" (`dennis2022common`). The standard published critique. It replicates Azar et al. and argues that the measured relationship is driven by the market-share component of the common-ownership measure rather than by ownership and control, using placebo versions of the measure; results also depend on how investor control is measured and on assumptions about control during bankruptcy.
- **Azar, Schmalz and Tecu (2022)**, "A Refutation of 'Common Ownership Does Not Have Anti-Competitive Effects in the Airline Industry'" (`azar2022refutation`). The reply, an unreviewed working paper, argues that the placebo tests are invalid because they are applied to an unbalanced panel in which the placebo is strongly negatively correlated with market shares; the dispute is unresolved as far as the notes found.
- **Indonesia (1999)**, Law No. 5 of 1999 on the Prohibition of Monopolistic Practices and Unfair Business Competition (`indonesia1999undang`). Per search summaries, Article 26 covers concurrent director or commissioner posts in other companies and Article 27 covers share ownership in competing companies, both under specified conditions and both in the abuse-of-dominant-position chapter. Being conditional rules, they do not make an observed interlock a breach; the article wording was not read from the official text.

## Pre-registration

- **Olken (2015)**, "Promises and Perils of Pre-analysis Plans" (`olken2015promises`). Weighs the benefits and costs of strictly pre-specifying variables, data cleaning and regression specifications; pre-specification greatly reduces data-mining concerns if all pre-specified results must be reported, but has real drawbacks. The paper is written mainly with randomized controlled trials in mind.
- **Burlig (2018)**, "Improving transparency in observational social science research: A pre-analysis plan approach" (`burlig2018improving`). Argues that pre-registration is feasible for observational work in three scenarios (researchers collecting their own data, prospective studies where the data do not yet exist, and restricted-access data) and proposes the contents of an observational pre-analysis plan. The prospective scenario fits a plan registered before the outcome data are pulled.
