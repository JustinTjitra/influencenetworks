## 1. Political connections and firm value

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings

**Fisman (2001)**
- Raymond Fisman. "Estimating the Value of Political Connections." *American Economic Review*, vol. 91, September 2001, pp. 1095-1102. VERIFIED-SNIPPET (Columbia Business School research listing; the issue number "4" was not stated in the snippet). — [Columbia GSB listing (test-domain mirror)](https://a.drupalgsb-test.paas.cc.columbia.edu/faculty/research/estimating-value-political-connections)
- Method and finding: an event-study approach on Indonesian firms, exploiting the fact that Indonesia's centralised and stable political structure under Suharto allows "a credible index of political connectedness"; conclusion that "a large percentage of a well-connected firm's value may be derived from political connections". `[memory]`: the events are rumours about Suharto's health, and connected firms fell more than unconnected ones, more so on days with larger market falls. — [Columbia-hosted PDF of the article](https://drupalgsb-test.paas.cc.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/1597/estimating_the_value.pdf)

**Faccio (2006)**
- Mara Faccio. "Politically Connected Firms." *American Economic Review*, 2006, vol. 96, issue 1, pp. 369-386. VERIFIED-HANDLE. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:aea:aecrev:v:96:y:2006:i:1:p:369-386)
- Method and finding: cross-country documentation of firms in 47 countries whose controlling shareholders or top officers overlap with legislators or government officials; connections are more common in more corrupt countries, and the announcement of a new political connection is associated with a significant increase in firm value. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:aea:aecrev:v:96:y:2006:i:1:p:369-386)

**Faccio, Masulis and McConnell (2006)**

- Method and finding: compares bailout likelihood for 450 politically connected firms from 35 countries over 1997-2002 with matched non-connected peers; connected firms are significantly more likely to be bailed out, especially when the IMF or World Bank is assisting the home government, and bailed-out connected firms perform worse than bailed-out unconnected ones. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v61y2006i6p2597-2635.html)

**Johnson and Mitton (2003)**
- Simon Johnson and Todd Mitton. "Cronyism and capital controls: evidence from Malaysia." *Journal of Financial Economics*, 2003, vol. 67, no. 2, pp. 351-382. DOI: 10.1016/S0304-405X(02)00255-6. VERIFIED-SNIPPET (two library/bibliographic records; not a RePEc handle). — [SUFE bibliographic record](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/357728); [Primo catalogue entry](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_38408151)

**Mobarak and Purbasari (2005/2006)**
- A. Mushfiq Mobarak and Denni Puspa Purbasari. "Political Trade Protection in Developing Countries: Firm Level Evidence from Indonesia." 2005 version, authors at University of Colorado at Boulder, presented at the NBER Summer Institute 2005 (ITI). VERIFIED-SNIPPET for this title and version only (authors' full first names are `[memory]`). — [NBER conference PDF](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2005/si2005/iti/mobarak.pdf)

**Leuz and Oberholzer-Gee (2006)**
- Christian Leuz and Felix Oberholzer-Gee. "Political relationships, global financing, and corporate transparency: Evidence from Indonesia." *Journal of Financial Economics*, 2006, vol. 81, issue 2, pp. 411-439. - Daron Acemoglu, Simon Johnson, Amir Kermani, James Kwak and Todd Mitton. - Method and finding: with event-date clustering, even relatively low cross-correlation among abnormal returns causes serious over-rejection of the null of zero mean abnormal return; the paper adjusts the Boehmer-Musumeci-Poulsen t-statistic for cross-correlation, which performs well against alternatives including the portfolio approach and extends to multi-day CARs. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v23y2010i11p3996-4025.html)

## 6. Pre-registration in observational finance/economics

### Takeaway
Olken (2015) and Burlig (2018) are both confirmed as the standard pair: Olken on costs and benefits of pre-analysis plans (mainly for RCTs), Burlig on extending pre-analysis plans to observational data. Neither DOI appeared in search results.
